## About me

I’m **Kang Chengkai (康城恺)**, and I go by **Kayel** on GitHub. I entered Wuhan University in 2024, started in the Physics Base Class, and joined the Yan Jici Physics Class in December 2025. I received a National Scholarship for Undergraduates. [About the Yan Jici Physics Class](https://iop.cas.cn/xwzx/snxw/202311/t20231123_6912635.html)

My first semester made me rethink how I studied. At UC Berkeley in summer 2025, working through discrete mathematics and abstract algebra with classmates showed me how much I could learn by comparing approaches to a problem. I took that habit into the China Undergraduate Physics Tournament. When we measured unexpected peaks in the sound spectrum of a vibrating ruler, my teammates and I used them to revise our model.

An English-taught optics course drew me toward light–matter interactions. At Wuhan University, I began studying the magneto-optical properties of two-dimensional magnetic semiconductors. During a research internship at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, I worked on low-temperature photoluminescence and electrostatic modeling of CrSBr devices. Those experiences led me to explore how physics and computation can help us understand neural signals, and to develop an interest in neuromorphic computing.

## Why I started this archive

I remember how much time I spent figuring out where to start, which prerequisites to revisit, and how to connect a derivation to a problem I could solve. Conversations with classmates helped me get unstuck. I want later students to have a place to find that kind of help even when we never meet. My aim is to pass on the notes and explanations I would have wanted when I first took these courses.

## How the archive is organized

I add materials by course so readers can find the relevant review guide, worked examples, or notes in one place. The availability table links directly to each resource as it is published.

## Contributing

If you spot a mistake or an explanation that skips a step, please open an issue with the course name and the relevant section. Pull requests with corrections, original notes, or worked examples are welcome. If you took a different version of a course, include the term so readers can tell which version the material covers.
